Pragmatic Lakehouse Architecture

A Governed, Interoperable, AI-Ready Enterprise Data Framework

The Pragmatic Lakehouse Architecture (PLA) is a governed, interoperable, AI-ready enterprise data framework built on three pillars: the METAllion Pattern — a metadata-governed, multi-zone data architecture pattern; the CDV Principle — an AI-first, AI-ready data strategy principle based on Converge, Diverge, and Virtualize (CDV); and PLA Open Blueprints — an agile, community-driven implementation guide that recognizes one size does not fit all. Together, these three pillars make every data product and every AI agent trustworthy by design.

One report. Seven o’clock. Every morning.

Your CEO has a simple request. He wants a Daily Sales Report — one page, on his phone, every morning by seven. Total revenue across all business units, broken down by region and product line. Yesterday’s actuals against the plan.

Eight weeks later, it still does not exist. If you have been in this situation, you know exactly how it happened. Not because your team lacks capability. Not because the data does not exist somewhere. But because the same metric means three different things across three business units. Two of those definitions live in Snowflake. One lives in Databricks. The European subsidiary reports under a recognition policy that differs from the US entity. The Asian operation, acquired eighteen months ago, calculates sales against a product hierarchy that does not map cleanly to yours. Nobody has agreed on exchange rates, reporting periods, or which inter-company transactions to eliminate.

Your AI team offered to help. They built an agent to automate the report. The agent ran. It returned a number — wrong enough that the CFO noticed on day one. The agent had no context for what the metric means in this organization, which inter-company transactions to exclude, or which recognition policy applies to the European entity. Querying raw tables, it returned technically accurate results that were semantically meaningless.

And now — the week you are supposed to deliver the report — your board has approved a merger with a competitor twice your size. The combined leadership team wants a consolidated view within thirty days of close. Your counterpart at the merging company runs entirely on Snowflake. Their definitions, their hierarchies, their fiscal calendar: none of it aligns with yours. Day one of the merger is six weeks away.

Rethinking the architecture

The data engineering community has made remarkable progress over the past decade. Modern lakehouse platforms are capable, open standards have matured, and governance tooling has caught up. Yet fragmentation, semantic inconsistency, and the challenge of keeping pace with organizational change persist across enterprises of every size and maturity. The reason is architectural, not technological — the pieces exist, but they have not been assembled into one coherent system designed for the reality of how enterprises actually operate. And now AI has arrived, amplifying every gap in the data estate it runs on.

The Pragmatic Lakehouse Architecture is a response to that reality — shaped not by theory, but by the experience of implementing data architecture in enterprises where the platforms are multiple, the regulatory constraints are real, and the business cannot wait for the architecture to be perfect before it needs answers. It is built on three pillars. First, an architectural pattern that defines how data is organized and governed across zones. Second, a data strategy principle that determines how data flows and how AI and security are embedded from the ground up. Third, an open blueprint guide built from real-world implementation experience. Each addresses a distinct dimension of the problem, and together they form one coherent system.

Pillar 1 — METAllion Pattern

METAllion — a zone-based pattern that reflects how enterprise data actually works

Enterprise data architecture has long organized data into progressive stages — raw ingestion, cleaning and conformation, and business-ready aggregation for consumption. The Medallion architecture, introduced by Databricks, formalized this into three named layers: Bronze, where raw data lands exactly as received; Silver, where data is cleaned and conformed; and Gold, where business-ready aggregates are assembled. The principle is sound and widely adopted.

The naming, however, carries an unintended implication. Medallion evokes a prize podium — Gold the first prize, Silver the second, Bronze the third — suggesting a hierarchy of value. In practice every layer is irreplaceable. AI and ML workloads depend on raw Bronze data. Compliance teams trace back to Bronze source records. Data scientists work directly with Silver. No layer is expendable, and the prize-podium metaphor fails to communicate this.

METAllion addresses this directly, and differently. Where Medallion has layers, METAllion has zones — each with its own purpose, its own ownership, and its own consumer communities, none ranked above the others. The META prefix names what Medallion left implicit: metadata governance is not a feature of the architecture but the architecture itself. Schema contracts, lineage graphs, data contracts, catalog registrations, and master data and reference data conformance rules run through every zone as the connective tissue that makes the pattern trustworthy, interoperable, and AI-ready.

METAllion proposes a five-zone pattern. It shares the foundational principle of progressive data quality improvement with Medallion and adopts three of its zone names, while introducing two brand new zones — the Copper zone, which standardizes data against enterprise-agreed rules before domain teams touch it, and the Platinum zone, which federates across all Gold zones for enterprise-level cross-domain consumption and is purposeful rather than mandatory.

ZoneWhat it does
BronzeRaw, immutable, complete. The permanent audit record of what arrived. Source for data engineers and compliance audit trails.
CopperEnterprise-agreed physical standardization enforced once, centrally. The starting point for AI, ML, and all domain teams. Mandatory.
SilverDomain curation. Business rules, joins, deduplication, domain-specific business transformation. Published as data assets with signed data contracts.
GoldAggregated data products. SLA-backed, tested, versioned. Serves domain consumers directly and feeds Platinum for enterprise needs.
PlatinumGoverned federation across all Gold zones. Enterprise metric definitions and cross-domain consistency. Purposeful, not mandatory.

The Copper zone — standardization before domain teams begin

At enterprise scale, the absence of a dedicated standardization zone means every domain team independently implements the same conformance rules — each making subtly different decisions that compound into inconsistencies across every downstream zone. Copper exists to solve this once, centrally, before domain teams begin — handling physical standardization so that Silver can focus entirely on domain-specific business transformation.

Source of authorityWhat Copper enforces
Schema enforcementCorrect physical types for every field. File-based sources often arrive as strings. Connector-sourced types are validated against the enterprise standard.
Enterprise catalogField classifications and physical format rules from Atlan, Alation, Collibra, or equivalent. The catalog is the specification. Copper is the enforcement.
Master dataProduct Master, Vendor Master, Customer Master, organizational hierarchies. Incoming identifiers validated as recognized, active entities before any domain team receives the data.
Reference dataCountry codes, currency codes, status lists, unit of measure standards. Where formal RDM exists, the enterprise list is the authority. Where absent, international standards (ISO 4217, ISO 3166, ISO 8601) serve as the baseline.

Copper and Silver serve fundamentally different purposes. Copper asks: does this data conform to what the enterprise has already agreed is true? Silver asks: what does this data mean within this domain, and what business rules apply? Copper rules are decided once, centrally, for everyone; Silver rules are owned by the team accountable for them. Copper does not add cost — it relocates it. Without Copper, every Silver pipeline independently pays the conformance cost with different outcomes. The cost Copper prevents — errors discovered silently in production dashboards — dwarfs any processing overhead.

AI has its most tractable role at Copper. Field classification at enterprise scale is precisely the pattern recognition work AI accelerates. An AI-assisted pipeline profiles Bronze data, generates conformance rules from the catalog and master and reference data, and flags exceptions for human approval. AI participates in building the data estate at Copper, then reads from Copper upward as a governed consumer — the architecture is AI-ready before the first model is trained.

The Platinum zone — governed federation for enterprise consumption

Gold zones serve domain consumers well. A Finance Gold zone delivers accurate P&L data for Finance. A Sales Gold zone delivers accurate pipeline data for Sales. Each is correct within its own context. But when the CEO asks for total revenue across all business units, no single Gold zone can answer that question — and asking each domain team to agree on one definition at query time is where eight-week delays originate. Platinum exists to solve this once, permanently. It federates across all Gold zones through shortcuts, mirroring, and live federation, presenting one governed consumption surface without moving data. Enterprise metric definitions — calculation logic, entity scope, recognition policy, currency treatment — are agreed once and encoded in Platinum. Every executive dashboard and every cross-domain report inherits that definition automatically. Governance is federated: domain analytics teams own metric definitions for their domain, and a cross-domain process resolves what must be consistent across all of them.

The second problem Platinum solves is more recent and more consequential. AI agents cannot operate on table and column names alone. An agent asked why revenue declined in APAC last quarter needs to understand what revenue means in this organization, which entities are in scope, what the fiscal calendar treatment is, and how the regional hierarchy maps to legal entities. Without that context, the agent returns a technically accurate number that is semantically meaningless. The Platinum zone is where that context lives. Through a knowledge graph that encodes enterprise metric definitions, entity relationships, organizational hierarchies, and business rules, AI agents can traverse context rather than just retrieve data. They inherit the organization’s agreed answer — with the full reasoning chain that makes it explainable.

Pillar 2 — CDV Principle

Converge, Diverge, Virtualize — an AI-First, AI-Ready data strategy principle

Converge → Diverge → Virtualize (CDV) is the data strategy principle that determines how data moves through the METAllion zones, who owns it at each stage, and how AI and security are embedded from the ground up.

This principle did not emerge in a vacuum. Two established frameworks shaped its thinking. Data Mesh, introduced by Zhamak Dehghani, established domain ownership and data as a product. Data Fabric, as defined by Gartner, established unified, platform-neutral access through active metadata. The CDV Principle brings the best of both — the centralized governance of Data Fabric where it is needed, and the domain autonomy of Data Mesh where it belongs.

PhaseZonesGoverning philosophy
Converge Bronze, Copper Data Fabric — unified access, centralized governance, one catalog, one standard applied to all sources. Security classified at Bronze and propagated downstream.
Diverge Silver, Gold Data Mesh — domain ownership, data as a product, signed data contracts, full domain accountability. Security enforced through inherited classification.
Virtualize Platinum Governed federation, enterprise metric definitions, virtualization first. One governed security surface across all Gold zones. AI agents inherit enterprise definitions.

Pillar 3 — PLA Open Blueprints

One framework. Many realities.

The METAllion Pattern and the CDV Principle together address the foundational questions of enterprise data architecture — what the zones are, who owns them, how data flows, how security travels, and where AI fits. What they cannot control is the estate you are asked to apply them to — legacy platforms that cannot be retired, regulatory regimes that conflict across jurisdictions, governance tooling that lags the architecture it is supposed to support, and organizations that merge before their data does. The third pillar exists because in enterprise data architecture, these are not exceptions. They are the operating conditions.

PLA Open Blueprints are distilled from real implementation experience — named patterns for specific real-world constraints, each grounded in the CDV Principle, each preserving the integrity of the METAllion Pattern while accommodating the reality it operates in.

Blueprint 1 — BI Directly on Gold
The Scenario

Not every analytics requirement needs enterprise-wide consistency. Domain-specific BI — Supply Chain reporting on its own inventory, Finance analyzing its own P&L — is best served at the Gold zone that owns the data. Routing everything through Platinum adds governance overhead without adding value for these consumers.

The Pattern

Build the consumption layer directly on the relevant Gold zone for domain-specific BI requirements. For AI agents, however, the calculus is different. Even a domain-specific AI agent benefits from the governed metric definitions and business context that Platinum provides — without that context, an agent querying Gold directly may return technically accurate results that are semantically meaningless. The recommendation is to route AI agent queries through Platinum regardless of whether the requirement is domain-specific or enterprise-wide. Reserve direct Gold access for human-facing BI and operational applications where domain accuracy is sufficient.

Key Principle: Domain-specific BI at Gold. AI agents and enterprise consumption at Platinum.
Blueprint 2 — Technology Limitations on Virtualization
The Scenario

Data platform vendors are moving fast. Virtualization and federation capabilities that did not exist two years ago are now shipping in major releases, and the gap between architectural intent and platform capability is closing. In the current state, however, some combinations still carry technical constraints. Snowflake to Microsoft Fabric mirroring, for example, replicates data internally rather than federating it in place — it is not zero-copy. Mirroring cannot be applied to views, only to tables. These are today’s limitations, not permanent architectural boundaries.

The Pattern

Apply virtualization where technically possible. Where data movement is unavoidable, govern it explicitly — audit the replication, minimize its scope, and treat it as a managed exception. Document the constraint and revisit as platform capabilities mature. Never allow a technical limitation to silently become an architectural norm.

Key Principle: Virtualization first. Governed replication as the explicit, audited exception.
Blueprint 3 — Catalog Maturity Gap
The Scenario

The Copper zone is driven by enterprise catalog classifications. Many organizations do not yet have a formal catalog — Atlan, Alation, Collibra, or equivalent — mature enough to drive Copper rule generation systematically.

The Pattern

Build a custom rule library for the Copper zone. The rule library is versioned, centrally governed, and maintained independently of any pipeline. International standards (ISO 4217, ISO 3166, ISO 8601) serve as the baseline for reference data. As catalog tooling matures, the library migrates into the catalog without pipeline rebuilds. In the absence of a catalog, a governed rule library is the catalog.

Key Principle: Where the catalog is not yet ready, rules belong in a governed library — not in code.
Blueprint 4 — Global, Regional, and Local Data Processing
The Scenario

Organizations operating across multiple jurisdictions face data residency, sovereignty, and localization requirements that prevent storing all data in one place. Depending on the regulatory framework and organizational structure, domain teams may require Silver and Gold zones to remain within regional boundaries — not just Bronze and Copper.

The Pattern

Deploy Bronze and Copper regionally in every jurisdiction — operated by the same central team, to the same governance standards, visible in the same global lineage graph. Silver and Gold may be deployed regionally where domain ownership is organized by region, regulatory requirements demand it, or data residency applies to processed data as well as raw. Where regulations permit, Silver and Gold may be global. The Platinum zone always federates across regional and global Gold zones through governed federation without the underlying data crossing jurisdictional boundaries it is not permitted to cross.

Key Principle: Global governance standard. Deployment topology follows the data residency requirement — not the other way around.
Blueprint 5 — Compliance and Data Security
The Scenario

Regulatory frameworks — SOX, GxP, HIPAA, GDPR, CCPA — impose specific data handling, residency, and access requirements. The CDV Principle embeds security as a metadata contract that travels with the data. This blueprint addresses how that contract is implemented and enforced across different regulatory contexts.

The Pattern

Apply physical or logical separation based on the specific regulatory requirement of each data type and jurisdiction. Physical separation — dedicated Bronze zone in dedicated storage within the correct jurisdiction — applies where regulations require storage isolation (GDPR personal data, HIPAA PHI, GxP electronic records, sovereignty laws). Logical separation applies where the requirement is access control rather than physical residency (SOX, confidential commercial data): RBAC for role-based controls, ABAC for attribute-based policies where data sensitivity varies by field value, column-level masking for personally identifiable fields, and row-level security for data products where consumer entitlements vary by record. Classification tags applied at Bronze propagate automatically through every downstream zone via the metadata contract. No zone configures access separately.

Key Principle: Classify at Bronze. Propagate through metadata. Enforce consistently at every zone.
Blueprint 6 — Multi-Platform Estates
The Scenario

Most enterprises operate on more than one data platform — Snowflake, Databricks, BigQuery, or combinations of all three. Migration to a single platform is rarely feasible in the near term. Platform proliferation is not a temporary problem. It is the permanent operating condition.

The Pattern

Govern multi-platform as a permanent condition through open standards. Data written on the primary platform is immediately readable on the secondary through open table format interoperability — Delta UniForm, Apache Iceberg — without ETL pipelines or unnecessary data movement. Share data intra-zone and inter-zone through open standards. Do not make copies. The Platinum zone federates across platforms through shortcuts and live federation, presenting one governed consumption surface regardless of which platform the underlying Gold zones run on.

Key Principle: Open standards over forced migration. Interoperability over consolidation.
Blueprint 7 — Mergers and Acquisitions: Governed Combined View on Day 2
The Scenario

A merger brings two data estates, two platform choices, two sets of metric definitions, and two organizational data cultures. Leadership needs a consolidated view within days of close. Infrastructure consolidation takes months. The gap between what leadership needs and what engineering can deliver is where trust breaks down.

The Pattern

Use the Platinum zone to federate across both companies’ Gold zones from Day 2. Open-standard interoperability (Blueprint 6) makes the acquired company’s Gold zone readable alongside the acquiring company’s without data movement or pipeline rebuilds. Enterprise metric definitions — one revenue definition, one regional hierarchy, one fiscal calendar treatment — are agreed and encoded in Platinum. Leadership sees one governed combined view on Day 2. Both companies do not need to be at the same level of METAllion implementation for this to work. Platinum federates across Gold zones regardless of whether the underlying estate has a Copper zone or not. Zone-level adoption — introducing Copper into the acquiring company’s estate, for example — can be phased as part of the post-merger integration journey, without disrupting the governed view that Platinum already provides. Governance arrived first and consolidation follows at its own pace.

Key Principle: Federate first in Platinum. Consolidate later. Leadership cannot wait for engineering.
Blueprint 8 — Ingestion Patterns for the Bronze Zone
The Scenario

Every data source that enters the METAllion pattern arrives at the Bronze zone. Not all sources are equal — some support CDC, some do not; some require near-real-time ingestion, others tolerate daily batch loads; some are large datasets requiring partitioned snapshots, others are small reference tables. Choosing the wrong ingestion pattern adds cost, latency, or complexity without adding value. Choosing the right one makes Bronze reliable and predictable for every downstream zone.

The Pattern

Ingestion pattern selection follows the characteristics of the source, not the preference of the engineering team. Four factors drive the decision: CDC availability, dataset size, latency requirement, and refresh tolerance. The decision tree below maps each combination to the appropriate pattern. The pattern selected at Bronze determines the SLA the Copper zone can commit to and the freshness guarantee the domain team inherits at Silver.

Key Principle: Ingestion pattern selection is an architectural decision, not an engineering preference. The wrong pattern at Bronze propagates the wrong SLA through every downstream zone.
Blueprint 9 — Knowledge Graph at Platinum
The Scenario

AI agents querying the Platinum zone inherit governed metric definitions and access controls. But metric definitions alone are not sufficient for complex reasoning. An agent asked why revenue declined in a region needs to traverse relationships — between entities, hierarchies, fiscal periods, and recognition policies — not just retrieve a number. Without a connected representation of enterprise meaning, the agent returns technically accurate but contextually incomplete answers.

The Pattern

The Platinum zone is the natural home for a knowledge graph — a connected representation of enterprise metric definitions, entity relationships, organizational hierarchies, and business context that AI agents can traverse and reason over rather than simply query. The graph is assembled from what already exists across the estate: the enterprise catalog contributes field classifications and business glossary terms; data contracts from Gold zones contribute domain semantics; master data contributes entity relationships; and lineage from Bronze through Gold contributes the provenance chain. Each domain contributes its subgraph and the cross-domain governance process connects them at the enterprise level — federated in ownership, unified in what it presents to AI consumers. An AI agent traversing the knowledge graph can follow relationships, resolve ambiguity between domain definitions, and return answers with full lineage of how the answer was constructed. That is what makes AI outputs not just accurate but explainable.

Key Principle: The Platinum zone does not just govern what data means. It makes that meaning traversable — so AI agents can reason over enterprise context, not just retrieve it.
Blueprint 10 — Data Contracts Between Zones
The Scenario

At enterprise scale, data products are consumed across zone boundaries by teams and systems that had no part in producing them. Any undocumented assumption about schema, quality, or delivery cadence becomes a silent dependency. When those assumptions break — and they do — the failure surfaces not at the source but at the dashboard, the report, or the AI agent that depended on it.

The Pattern

A data contract is a formal, versioned agreement between a data product producer and its consumers. It specifies the schema, the SLA, the data quality expectations, the ownership, and the terms under which the contract can be changed. In METAllion, contracts operate at two zone boundaries where ownership changes hands. At the Copper-to-Silver boundary, the central team hands off to the domain team. The contract makes the standardization guarantee explicit — what has been enforced at Copper, what the domain team inherits, and what they are now accountable for. Without this contract, the boundary between universal rules and domain rules remains implicit and unenforceable. At the Gold-to-Platinum boundary, the domain team hands off to the federated enterprise consumption surface. The contract governs how domain data products are exposed at the enterprise level, what the SLA commitment is, and what changes require cross-domain governance approval before they can be promoted. Changes to either contract require versioning, consumer notification, and a deprecation period — the producer cannot silently break the consumer.

Key Principle: A data product without a contract is a dependency without a guarantee. Contracts make the implicit explicit and the undocumented enforceable.

Back to seven o’clock

The CEO still wants his report. Eight weeks in, two platforms, one impending merger, one AI agent that embarrassed the CFO. The three pillars address each directly.

The fragmented data estate. The Copper zone enforces enterprise catalog, master data, and reference data conformance before any domain team touches the data. Every domain — Finance, Sales, the European subsidiary, the acquired Asian operation — starts from the same standardized foundation. The Platinum zone defines the enterprise metric once: calculation logic, entities, inter-company eliminations, recognition policy. Every consumer inherits that definition. There is no negotiation at report time. The governance happened upstream, in the zones.

The AI agent that got it wrong. The CDV Principle makes AI-readiness an architectural property, not a configuration. The agent queries the Platinum zone and inherits one enterprise-agreed definition — correct exclusions, correct recognition policy, correct access controls already applied. The agent did not become smarter — the architecture became governed.

The merger on the horizon. The PLA Open Blueprints apply directly — specifically the M&A blueprint. Open-standard interoperability makes the merger counterpart’s Snowflake Gold zone readable alongside yours without moving data. The Platinum zone federates both into one governed combined view within days of close. Leadership sees the consolidated picture before a single pipeline is rebuilt. The METAllion Pattern, the CDV Principle, and the PLA Open Blueprints working together — the report exists, it is governed, and it will be there every morning by seven.
